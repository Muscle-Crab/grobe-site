Tips from the Grobe team

Meal photo logging tips, made simple

We built Grobe so you could snap your food instead of typing it. Here are a few things we've learned about getting good results, and about reading what the app shows you.

1. Take a meal photo the AI can read

  • Shoot from above or at a slight angle so every item on the plate is visible.
  • Use good light. Daylight or a bright room beats a dim restaurant.
  • Keep foods from hiding each other. If something is under a sauce, mention it when you review.
  • Include something for scale, like a fork or your hand, so portions are easier to judge.
  • Always check the result before you log it, and fix anything the AI missed.

A photo estimate is an estimate. Oils, dressings and hidden ingredients are the hardest part for any camera, so a quick review goes a long way.

2. Reading your macros

Macros are the three nutrients that give your food its calories:

  • Protein has about 4 calories per gram. It helps build and repair your body.
  • Carbs have about 4 calories per gram. They are your body's main quick fuel.
  • Fat has about 9 calories per gram, which is why small amounts of oil add up fast.

In Grobe, your daily ring shows how a meal fits into your day. One meal over or under doesn't matter much. The pattern across a week tells you more.

3. Making sense of vitamins and minerals

Micronutrients don't have calories, but they matter. Grobe shows things like iron, calcium, potassium, magnesium, zinc, folate and vitamins A, C, D and B12 for each meal.

  • Look for gaps that keep showing up over several days, not just one meal.
  • Colorful plates, beans, nuts, greens and fish tend to cover a lot of ground.
  • Before taking supplements, talk to a doctor or pharmacist.

4. Make it a habit, not a chore

Snap before you eat, even when the meal isn't perfect. Honest logs are more useful than pretty ones. If you like a little push, the monthly Health Challenge gives you XP for healthy meals and resets on the 1st.
